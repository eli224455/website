The firm

A law office in Addis Ababa for individuals, families and businesses

Most people who need a lawyer did not expect to. A marriage ends. A parent dies and the family cannot agree about the house. A supplier stops delivering and a business is suddenly exposed. Someone is arrested, and the family has hours rather than days to respond.

Elizabeth Tesfaye Law Office exists for those moments. We practise across six areas of Ethiopian law — family law, civil litigation, succession and inheritance, business and corporate law, criminal defense, and general legal consultation — from our office in Summit, Addis Ababa.

That breadth is deliberate rather than accidental. Legal problems seldom arrive in a single tidy category: a divorce raises questions about a family business, an inheritance dispute turns on a property transfer made years earlier, a commercial disagreement escalates into a criminal complaint. Handling related matters within one office means you are not sent elsewhere at the point where continuity matters most.

What clients tell us they value is simpler than legal skill, and rarer: being told plainly what the law provides, what is likely to happen, roughly how long it will take and what it will cost — before committing to anything.

Before you call

Questions clients ask first

How much does a consultation cost?

It depends on the matter, and we will tell you before the work begins rather than afterwards. For consultations, document review and written legal opinions we can usually quote a fixed fee in advance. For litigation we agree the fee basis at the outset and confirm it in writing.

I live abroad. Can you handle my matter without me travelling to Ethiopia?

In most cases, yes. We act regularly for clients in the diaspora. Documents are exchanged by email and consultations held by telephone or video call. Steps that must be taken here — filing a case, obtaining a certificate of heir, transferring property — can generally be done under a properly executed and authenticated power of attorney. We will tell you exactly what needs signing, and where it needs authenticating, before you incur any expense.

How is property divided in a divorce in Ethiopia?

The Revised Family Code distinguishes between each spouse's personal property and the common property of the marriage. Property acquired during the marriage is generally presumed common unless the spouse claiming it proves otherwise — which places the burden of proof on them. In practice these disputes are decided by documentary evidence of when and how each asset was acquired, which is why we start with your documents rather than your account of events.

A family member has been arrested. What should I do?

Contact a lawyer immediately — what happens in the first days of a criminal matter frequently shapes everything that follows. Note where the person is held and which police station has the file, and avoid discussing the substance of the allegation by telephone. Under the Constitution an arrested person has the right to be told the reason for arrest, the right to legal representation, the right not to be compelled to incriminate themselves, and the right to be brought before a court within a short and defined period.
